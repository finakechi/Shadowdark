# Complete class-kit evaluation — twelve-class adaptation

Status: Proposed design evaluation (SD-022); no revised class kit is accepted or ready for play.  
Version: 0.1  
Research / evaluation date: 2026-10-24 (UTC)  
Scope decisions: SD-019 roster Accepted; SD-021 Rogue/Cleric names Accepted.  
Related: [roster](class-roster.md), [shared framework](class-design-framework.md), [HB-002](../rules/homebrew/hb-002-character-options.md), [magic](../rules/homebrew/hb-003-magic-framework.md), [durability](../rules/homebrew/hb-004-durability-and-encounters.md), [decision log](../decisions/decision-log.md).

## Finding and scope

Recommend twelve coordinated adaptations, with **unequal amounts of redesign**. Fighter and Rogue need more accessible combat decisions. Cleric and Wizard already have substantial capability and need targeted choices plus utility/recovery review. The other eight need original complete foundations; their identity briefs are not existing mechanical kits we can pronounce balanced.

Here, a complete-kit evaluation examines equipment/defense, core actions, magical access, utility, resources, failure, progression and interactions together. It is a package-level audit and set of candidate kits, not final numerical class sheets, an exhaustive transcription of every D&D feature, or a playtest. All twelve receive the same review fields below. The exact triggers, numbers, lists and tables still need authoring.

## Common recommendation

Keep Shadowdark's action structure and risky casting as the initial comparison engine, while adding meaningful abilities early. This remains a proposal under SD-020/022. Do not stack the earlier HB-002 additive counts on top of these candidate packages automatically.

1. Each character should have a real level-1 operating choice and a useful contribution during freeform exploration/social play. A spell or existing expertise can satisfy a requirement; it need not be an extra named feature.
2. Whole-kit power is not proportional to feature count. Armor, spell breadth, passive protection, control and repeatable healing all count.
3. Start with normal actions and existing resources. A bespoke pool is justified only if it produces useful decisions better than a simpler alternative.
4. Ordinary exploration stays freeform. Short powers get explicit local timing; they do not force all activity into rounds. Dangerous darkness immediately invokes the accepted urgent-round procedure, restrictions and encounter cadence.
5. Retain possible failures and worthwhile successes. Some barriers should become easier because of a class choice. Do not nullify every new capability with immunity clauses.
6. No starting HP formula, recovery package, stat-generation change, talent replacement or enemy multiplier is selected here. Comparative dice below anchor a later discussion; the eight new assignments are Proposed and not published facts.
7. Keep the existing talent schedule as a comparison benchmark. Review level-3 combinations of talents, spell-tier growth and new chosen abilities before adding benefits. The proposed level-2/3 sketches are not accepted entitlements.

## Class-by-class findings

The short published summaries establish source context. All subsequent package recommendations are Proposed.

### Barbarian

**Published reference:** The 2024 package combines Rage, weapon mastery, unarmored defense, reckless offense, danger awareness, extra attacks and later resilience/control. Its power is a combination of damage, accuracy and survival, not Rage damage alone. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1750-2024-barbarian-vs-2014-barbarian-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No verified official Shadowdark Barbarian interior is available for this evaluation. Minimal alternative A is our own Fighter-derived bruiser with a fury feature, not a published class.

**Foundation:** STR-led; CON supports endurance. Broad melee and thrown weapons; leather/chain and shields as a proposed starting permission, with a reason to choose an unarmored style. Use d8 only as a comparative HP-die placeholder pending HB-004; do not import D&D's d12.

**Recommended level-1 package:** A commitment-based fury; a choice between a forceful attack and a defensive/positional option; physical expertise for breaking routes, hauling or surviving exposure. Fury should modify an existing action rather than add another attack. One chosen style can favor aggression or guarding a retreat.

**Magic and utility coverage:** No general spell list. Primal flavor can be mundane or supernatural without granting automatic detection, resistance or immunity. The class must remain satisfying without a casting resource.

**Progression:** Level 2 adds a new way to use commitment, not another always-on damage bonus. Level 3 differentiates aggression, endurance or battlefield disruption. Later growth improves selected effects and recovery from commitment; do not map D&D's extra-attack levels directly.

**Resources and action costs:** Prefer a visible tradeoff in exposure, mobility or objective commitment over rage-use counters plus upkeep rounds plus exhaustion. Compare a short use pool only if exposure makes the mechanic too binary. Never require routine freeform exploration to remain in rounds to sustain fury.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Broad damage resistance would multiply effective HP before our durability decision. Accuracy advantage plus specialization and damage bonuses can produce a dominant attack. Repeated knockdowns must not remove the target's actions indefinitely.

**Failure and adjudication:** State whether a missed committed attack leaves exposure in place. A failed breach can cost time, noise or position when those consequences were disclosed; toughness is not immunity to poison, drowning or darkness.

**Practical test:** A narrow retreat with an ally to protect, then a noisy physical barrier. Check whether aggression is always better and whether the Fighter still has the better control choices.

### Bard

**Published reference:** The 2024 Bard combines spellcasting, Inspiration, expertise, broad competence and later access to other classes' spell lists. Inspiration and spell resources also interact. Counting it as merely a support ability plus a few spells misses much of the package. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1768-2024-bard-vs-2014-bard-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No supplementary Shadowdark Bard rules have been verified in full here. Minimal A is an original narrow caster with simple encouragement, not a claim about an official Bard.

**Foundation:** CHA-led; light armor, selected light/ranged weapons and an instrument or other performance method. Comparative d6 placeholder. Performance can be speech, movement or music; avoid an instrument requirement that defeats a chosen concept.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Magical support with a timing choice; a deliberately bounded spell repertoire focused on influence, illusion and assistance; one chosen lore or social specialty. Support should work when direct mundane assistance cannot, so we do not sell the ordinary ability to help as a class feature.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Use a limited, curated roll-to-cast list as the initial direction. A few genuinely magical tricks belong in the kit; do not reduce the Bard to a nonmagical cheerleader. Broad healing and cross-list acquisition must compete with other choices rather than arrive free.

**Progression:** Level 2 expands support applications or a chosen specialty. Level 3 selects an emphasis such as lore, guile or battle support without silently receiving a complete D&D subclass. Later spell borrowing should require specific discoveries and a narrow choice.

**Resources and action costs:** Compare support that spends the Bard's action with a small shared-use resource for a stronger intervention. Do not stack a new pool, luck-token generation and casting refunds. Any prepared encouragement expires after its specified immediate opportunity; no banking benefits across an expedition.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Repeated accuracy support can greatly improve casting endurance. Universal expertise sidelines Rogue/Ranger, and full access to healing, control and every spell list risks covering several classes at once. Social magic can bypass factions more thoroughly than an attack bypasses a guard.

**Failure and adjudication:** Define whether a failed performance expends its resource and what witnesses perceive. Influence cannot dictate unlimited behavior; disclose what breaks it and when a target learns of magical interference.

**Practical test:** A tense negotiation, a failed critical attempt and a fight with separated allies. Check actual support frequency, whether the Bard has worthwhile solo actions, and whether another PC can still lead a conversation.

### Cleric

**Published reference:** The 2024 Cleric includes casting, an early sacred-role choice, Channel Divinity, undead opposition, later damage enhancements and Divine Intervention. Its armor, support and magic must be evaluated together. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1767-2024-cleric-vs-2014-cleric-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** Our verified Shadowdark Priest already has all armor/shields, a restricted weapon list, WIS casting, two chosen tier-1 spells plus Turn Undead, a sacred language and a deity. Renaming it Cleric changes none of these mechanics.

**Foundation:** Retain the Priest chassis as the comparison baseline: d6, existing weapon/armor permissions and WIS casting. Its existing armor is part of its power budget; do not count heavy armor as an additional imported upgrade.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Keep undead opposition and useful divine spells. Add an early devotional choice that changes how the character intervenes, plus bounded religious investigation or a rite. A protective and a restorative route should differ in decisions rather than merely bonus size.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Use the verified Priest casting engine and known-spell progression as the initial benchmark. Audit healing, Light, Protection From Evil, truth effects and later cleansing individually. Published Protection From Evil already interacts with possession; the investigation/ritual proposal does not silently remove that effect.

**Progression:** Level 2 deepens the chosen devotional practice or grants a distinct intervention. Level 3 grows spell access and specialization with attention to the existing talent roll. Later divine favors need specified effects; no unrestricted request for any desired miracle.

**Resources and action costs:** Casting failure remains a resource constraint. Additional healing must draw on an existing limit or replace another benefit. A rite can reveal a lead, suppress a specified symptom or establish a narrow ward; major curse removal follows the serious-cost requirement.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Healing plus armor plus protection can create a powerful attrition engine without exceptional damage. Removing or bypassing casting loss would be a major buff. Religious utility should not provide guaranteed answers or absolute safe resting places.

**Failure and adjudication:** Keep the distinction between ordinary casting loss and critical failure. If revising penance later, state the cost rather than inventing surprise obligations. A failed investigation should not erase known religious expertise.

**Practical test:** Several linked encounters with recovery opportunities, then a haunted site with both mundane and magical routes. Track healing attempts, spell loss, armor protection and whether diagnosis produces an actionable clue.

### Druid

**Published reference:** The 2024 Druid combines nature casting, Wild Shape, companion access and resource exchanges. Its 2024 forms retain the Druid's HP and grant temporary HP; they do not use the 2014 separate beast-HP model. Base-class forms and Moon-subclass benefits are distinct. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1755-the-2024-circle-of-the-moon-druid-and-changes-to).

**Shadowdark comparison:** We have not verified an official Shadowdark Druid's full rules. Minimal A is our nature-caster stand-in with very modest shape utility; it is not a published baseline.

**Foundation:** WIS-led; light armor/shields and a specified simple-weapon list as a proposed starting permission. Comparative d6 placeholder. Avoid importing a second ability-score dependency solely to reproduce D&D armor math.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Nature spells plus a small set of explicit form templates. Recommend a limited form-related choice at level 1 so the signature matters during a short campaign; this is our proposal, not D&D's starting level for Wild Shape. Offer ecology and creature communication that work in ruins, fungi caverns and subterranean water.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Fewer initial spell choices than the Wizard benchmark while forms provide real utility. Define senses, equipment, size, movement and permitted actions for each template. Nature magic does not imply all healing, every beast stat block, a familiar and broad elemental control at once.

**Progression:** Level 2 adds a form application or a different nature technique. Level 3 selects deeper forms or deeper spellcasting, with both retaining useful core identity. Flight, tiny infiltration and powerful senses require specific later review; later upgrades need not all increase combat statistics.

**Resources and action costs:** Recommend one shared magic limit for stronger transformations and spells, or a tightly bounded form-use limit; compare before choosing. No free HP refill on transforming. Reverting, injury, equipment and interruption need exact rules. Cosmetic changes can remain easy.

**Balance and encounter implications:** A small scout can bypass doors, traps, light and patrols even without dealing damage. Forms plus a companion can add more effective actions than the class sheet suggests. On-demand temporary HP can also become repeatable healing in practice.

**Failure and adjudication:** State what damage and incapacitation do to a form, whether an interrupted change spends its cost, and how a trapped form can revert. No undefined access through cracks merely because an animal sounds small.

**Practical test:** A barred passage, a dark scout route, a flooded chamber and a combat transition. Track solo spotlight time and whether allies still have meaningful ways to cross or investigate.

### Fighter

**Published reference:** The 2024 Fighter combines fighting style, weapon mastery, Second Wind, extra actions/attacks and later tactical and defensive improvements. Its package also has utility through Tactical Mind, not just combat throughput. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1746-2024-fighter-vs-2014-fighter-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** Shadowdark Fighter has all weapons/armor/shields, d8 HP, Hauler, a chosen weapon's scaling attack/damage bonus and a STR-or-DEX expertise benefit. Numerical talent growth already reinforces fighting reliability.

**Foundation:** Keep the verified chassis as the benchmark. Support STR or DEX concepts. Do not charge another feature for the existing physical expertise, carrying capacity or specialization.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Add a compact set of situational techniques tied to weapon use, positioning or protection. A technique should exchange damage, movement or an action for a benefit rather than append free control to every strike. Add concrete tactical assessment and expedition applications beyond repeating Grit.

**Magic and utility coverage:** No required spell list. A relic-bearing or supernatural concept can be considered later; the base Fighter must have strong choices through practical skill. Useful martial options should not depend on receiving a magic item.

**Progression:** Level 2 expands a technique or offers a different fighting approach. Level 3 combines existing talents with a chosen specialization in control, protection or precision. Later growth improves options and reliability; additional attacks are a separate change to the game's scale.

**Resources and action costs:** Prefer at-will conditional maneuvers with opportunity costs. A small exertion pool is an alternative if stronger effects are needed, not an automatic requirement. Second-Wind-like healing belongs in durability review, not a free addition here.

**Balance and encounter implications:** The existing specialization bonus stacks with new accuracy and damage. Repeated forced movement can be lethal near drops and can prevent enemies from acting; a control option's total value exceeds its printed damage. Covering an ally should require a defined position or commitment.

**Failure and adjudication:** State what an unsuccessful disarm, push or guard attempt costs. Other classes may still attempt reasonable maneuvers; Fighter techniques improve reliability, effect or efficiency rather than reserve imagination for Fighters.

**Practical test:** A chokepoint, an enemy guarding a lever and a retreat under pursuit. Check whether techniques beat attacking in every situation or never justify their cost.

### Monk

**Published reference:** The 2024 Monk combines Martial Arts, unarmored movement/defense, Focus-fueled choices, deflection, stun and later resilience. Bonus actions and recovery are important parts of its effectiveness; its attack count cannot be ignored in conversion. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1758-2024-monk-vs-2014-monk-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No official Shadowdark Monk interior was verified here. Minimal A is an original unarmed, mobile Fighter-derived stand-in.

**Foundation:** DEX-led; WIS may inform discipline and perception without making two high scores mandatory for basic competence. Specified unarmed/simple weapons; no armor/shield for discipline benefits. Comparative d6 placeholder. An explicit unarmored AC formula remains a drafting dependency.

**Recommended level-1 package:** A credible unarmed attack plus a small choice of techniques emphasizing strike, guard or movement. The player needs an interesting decision with one action, not a weaker version of D&D awaiting Flurry. Add bodily discipline, balance and quiet traversal with stated practical effects.

**Magic and utility coverage:** No general spell list initially. Supernatural movement or elemental disciplines can be selected directions, not universal gifts. Do not make being unarmed grant free climbing, unlimited breath, light independence and immunity to disease.

**Progression:** Level 2 improves a technique or adds a discipline. Level 3 differentiates mobile assault, protection or a bounded supernatural expression. Later movement upgrades should specify surfaces and limitations; stun is not the inevitable base-class destination.

**Resources and action costs:** Recommend small stance/technique choices first; compare one Focus pool if it creates genuinely different decisions. Stances must have clear entry/exit timing and cannot all be active. Do not add a reaction economy just to reproduce deflection.

**Balance and encounter implications:** A fragile melee kit may fail before its choices matter. Conversely, easy defense plus free disengagement plus multiple attacks can dominate. Repeatable stun removes enemy play and is especially strong against a solo threat.

**Failure and adjudication:** A failed leap still has an announced consequence; discipline should sometimes improve reliability without making the GM demand rolls for ordinary movement. Defensive techniques must specify whether they affect one attack or a whole round.

**Practical test:** A fight requiring movement through threatened space and an awkward vertical route. Track survivability, time spent choosing stances and whether control locks down enemies.

### Paladin

**Published reference:** The 2024 Paladin bundles martial equipment, spellcasting, Lay on Hands, smiting, Channel Divinity and later auras/steed features. Healing and party defenses matter as much as burst damage. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1749-2024-paladin-vs-2014-paladin-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No official Shadowdark Paladin-equivalent interior is verified here. Minimal A is an original armed Priest-derived champion; it does not combine complete Priest and Fighter benefits.

**Foundation:** STR or DEX martial lead with CHA for chosen oath powers as a proposed direction. All armor/shields and a defined martial weapon list; comparative d8 placeholder. Stat generation must be reviewed before making two high modifiers essential.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Choose a vow expression that gives a distinct protective or decisive intervention. Include a limited healing/stabilizing option and an oath-related information/social application. These benefits share a budget; smiting, healing and protection are not three free complete engines.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Recommend narrow, named oath miracles rather than the Cleric's whole spell progression plus Fighter specialization. A smite-like option replaces part of an attack benefit or spends a shared limit. No automatic mount, pervasive aura or broad spell list at level 1.

**Progression:** Level 2 adds an oath application or a choice between greater protection and offense. Level 3 strengthens the chosen identity rather than adding every missing pillar. Later aura design should use narrow triggers, targets and effects rather than constant bonuses to all checks.

**Resources and action costs:** Compare a shared small miracle allowance with roll-to-invoke powers. Avoid separate smite, healing, divinity and spell resources. Vow terms are declared during creation; breaking a vow follows a stated process, not retroactive loss of a character's kit.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Armor, good attacks, healing and aura bonuses can make the class the safest default choice. A bonus to an ally's casting check improves magic endurance as well as defense. Blanket fear/condition protection can erase planned kinds of danger.

**Failure and adjudication:** Specify when an invocation's cost is paid and whether the weapon hit still resolves if the miracle fails. Holy authority does not compel NPC obedience or reveal all moral truth.

**Practical test:** Protecting a vulnerable ally, deciding between offense and healing, then negotiating a conflicting obligation. Check whether Fighter/Cleric remain better at their own specialties.

### Ranger

**Published reference:** The 2024 Ranger includes weapon mastery, early casting, Hunter's Mark, expertise, mobility and later defensive/sensory improvements. Its combat and exploration tools form a hybrid package. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1759-2024-ranger-vs-2014-ranger-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No official Shadowdark Ranger interior is verified here. Minimal A is our tracking-focused Fighter stand-in. An official download listing alone would not establish its mechanics.

**Foundation:** STR or DEX-led with WIS for fieldcraft/magic. Broad ranged and selected melee weapons, leather/chain and optional shield tradeoffs; comparative d6 placeholder. Equipment and ability wording must support a melee Ranger too.

**Recommended level-1 package:** A pursuit/preparation choice that changes ambush, positioning or route decisions; strong tracking and hazard-reading; a narrow set of nature spells or field rites. The campaign's underground environment is a central application, not an exception to forest-only benefits.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Keep some real magic available early. Prefer selected fieldcraft spells over duplicating the Druid's forms or complete nature repertoire. Do not require a concentration/focus mark to make every normal attack competitive.

**Progression:** Level 2 broadens a field technique or a quarry application. Level 3 selects pursuit, ambush or expedition support while preserving basic tracking. Later senses and concealment need explicit limits; no automatic darkness solution.

**Resources and action costs:** Use time, evidence, route commitment and limited magic where meaningful. A quarry feature needs a clear way to identify/change its subject without constant remarking bookkeeping. Ordinary tracking should not consume a point just because it appears on a class sheet.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Perfect navigation, food production and encounter avoidance erase expedition decisions. Too much mark-based damage makes the feature mandatory and can displace utility casting. A companion adds action and spotlight costs and is not assumed in the base kit.

**Failure and adjudication:** Tracking should return information justified by the evidence; failure may leave uncertainty or cost time, not arbitrarily lead into an unavoidable ambush. A prepared route offers an advantage rather than guaranteeing safety.

**Practical test:** Following incomplete tracks through ruins, preparing an ambush and choosing between a short dangerous route and a longer exposed route. Test both bow and melee concepts.

### Rogue

**Published reference:** The 2024 Rogue combines Sneak Attack, expertise, action-efficient movement, defensive features and later Cunning Strike trades. Its repeatable qualifying damage must be considered alongside utility. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1752-2024-rogue-vs-2014-rogue-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** Shadowdark Thief already has broad trained thievery, concealed trade tools, d4 HP and Backstab against a target unaware of the attack. That trigger differs from D&D Sneak Attack; the approved Rogue name does not broaden it.

**Foundation:** DEX-led; retain the verified Thief weapon/armor permissions as the benchmark, including its existing mithral-chain allowance. Keep the existing tool and expertise benefits. Its d4 is a baseline comparison, not approval of final toughness.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Preserve expert infiltration and a rewarding ambush. Add a reliable combat choice involving distraction, escape or a temporary opening. Recommend keeping full Backstab tied to unawareness while separately designing a smaller opening effect or setup benefit, so useful combat play does not require full bonus damage every turn.

**Magic and utility coverage:** No required base spell list. Tricks, alchemy or a later magical specialty can be choices; do not assume every Rogue is an Arcane Trickster. Other characters retain ordinary stealth, lock interaction and plausible deception.

**Progression:** Level 2 expands an exploit or escape method. Level 3 differentiates infiltrator, saboteur or opportunist. Later talent growth already increases Backstab; inspect that growth before adding more dice or multiple qualifying attacks.

**Resources and action costs:** Position, setup and surrendered damage can pay for tactics. Do not repeatedly spend an entire turn merely to be allowed a modest attack next turn. Avoid free automatic hiding where the environment provides no concealment.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Changing Backstab to qualify whenever an ally is nearby greatly raises damage frequency even with unchanged dice. Repeated advantage, safe ranged attacks and defensive movement can stack. Expanding expertise into guaranteed success can eliminate meaningful mechanisms and social risks.

**Failure and adjudication:** State when a failed deception exposes the Rogue and whether a setup survives a miss. Thievery is competence, not trap immunity; give useful observations without charging a separate feature for every tool task.

**Practical test:** One fight beginning with an ambush and one with the party exposed, then a mechanism and a deception. Compare bonus-damage frequency and whether the exposed Rogue still has worthwhile choices.

### Sorcerer

**Published reference:** The 2024 Sorcerer combines casting, Innate Sorcery, Metamagic, resource conversion and later restoration. Spell reliability, modification and replenishment are interdependent benefits. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1769-2024-sorcerer-vs-2014-sorcerer-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** No official Shadowdark Sorcerer interior is verified here. Minimal A is an original CHA-based Wizard reskin without its own strong operating choice.

**Foundation:** CHA-led; dagger/staff and no armor as proposed baseline permissions, with comparative d4 placeholder. Theme-linked defenses are chosen abilities, not free compensation for foregoing armor.

**Recommended level-1 package:** A bounded innate repertoire, a chosen magical gift and a spell-shaping choice available early. Provide a noncombat expression of that same gift. Spell shaping should alter delivery or application, not simply grant more actions and damage.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Recommend a narrow known-spell list without the Wizard's open-ended scroll-learning benefit. Compare a casting-risk surcharge for stronger shaping against a small shared resource. Do not grant permanent advantage on all casting as the default expression of Innate Sorcery.

**Progression:** Level 2 expands shaping options; level 3 deepens the gift or a chosen modification. Later growth can expand combinations, but each interaction needs review. Do not convert all Sorcery Points and spell slots into a second economy beside spell loss.

**Resources and action costs:** A limited shaping rule should state when the choice is made, its additional risk/cost, and what failure loses. A narrow repertoire is a real constraint but must still offer useful fallback choices after one spell is lost.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Casting advantage changes expected successful uses before spell loss dramatically. Free silent/subtle magic can also eliminate social and stealth consequences. Additional targets, casting actions or durations can multiply effects beyond a modest numerical buff.

**Failure and adjudication:** Specify whether a shaped spell's critical failure uses an existing arcane mishap or a short tailored table; do not invent hidden extra punishments. A magical gift's minor application is not permission for arbitrary spell effects.

**Practical test:** The same spell used in a fight, a social scene and a traversal problem. Count whether one modification is always best, how often casting remains available, and whether Wizard breadth still matters.

### Warlock

**Published reference:** The 2024 Warlock combines Pact Magic, invocations, pact gifts, repeatable cantrip options and separate recovery features. Its invocations can include weapon, book and familiar benefits rather than a single permanently exclusive pact. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1756-2024-warlock-vs-2014-warlock-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** The publisher's Cursed Scroll 1 listing establishes an official Shadowdark Warlock exists, but its full interior has not been verified here. We are not evaluating or reproducing that unseen kit. Minimal A is an original narrow occult caster with one gift.

**Foundation:** CHA-led; light armor and specified simple weapons as a starting proposal, comparative d6 placeholder. A pact-weapon route changes selected permissions or attack use rather than receiving the full Fighter chassis.

**Recommended level-1 package:** One chosen pact gift, a narrow occult spell repertoire and a viable repeatable magical fallback. A bolt, pact weapon or other fallback uses the normal action and an explicit attack/check; no automatic multi-beam scaling plus control. Include occult interpretation or contact utility.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Use one common casting engine initially, with gifts providing the distinctive feel. A weapon, book or familiar route should trade benefits rather than arrive together. A familiar needs a control/action budget, sensory rules and replacement cost before selection.

**Progression:** Level 2 expands or refines the gift; level 3 makes a stronger pact expression available. Later secrets can be major ritual rewards. More gifts are possible only after reviewing their combined coverage; not every build needs a combat pet.

**Resources and action costs:** Avoid both spell slots and spell-loss tracking. Compare fixed gift tradeoffs with a shared invocation limit for stronger boons. Pact terms are disclosed and agreed; the GM does not suddenly demand payment after every routine cast.

**Balance and encounter implications:** At-will attack plus forced movement can dominate dangerous ledges. Free scouting, darkness sight, invisible companions and broad ritual books can remove expedition constraints. CHA weapon attacks also consolidate stats and expand social competence.

**Failure and adjudication:** Clarify whether a failed pact invocation loses a spell, temporarily locks a gift or has another stated effect. Loss of all class powers is not an unannounced narrative consequence. Spellcasting and companion replacement must not become unlimited safe retry loops.

**Practical test:** A light failure, a narrow bridge, an occult bargain and a familiar reconnaissance attempt if that route is drafted. Check damage/control repetition and how much scouting risk remains with the party.

### Wizard

**Published reference:** The 2024 Wizard combines learned casting, a spellbook, ritual access, recovery and later preparation flexibility/mastery. The number of useful available answers is a major part of the package. [Official class reference](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1753-2024-wizard-vs-2014-wizard-whats-new).

**Shadowdark comparison:** Shadowdark Wizard has d4 HP, dagger/staff, no armor, extra languages, three chosen tier-1 spells and a scroll-learning route beyond normal spell-knowledge limits. Its kit already provides substantial breadth.

**Foundation:** Keep that verified chassis and INT casting as the benchmark. Preserve rune/language competence and scroll acquisition. Do not charge a new feature to regain knowledge already supported by training.

**Recommended level-1 package:** Keep learned breadth and acquired spells. Add a chosen study emphasis or bounded practical technique that changes how the Wizard investigates or applies a known method. A small magical utility expression can help when spells are lost, but should be narrower than a free replacement spell.

**Magic and utility coverage:** Use the existing Wizard engine and progression initially. Curate which spells become rituals and which remain adventuring spells. Distinguish knowledge acquired over the campaign from an ability to prepare any answer immediately in the dungeon.

**Progression:** Level 2 adds a study application; level 3 combines new tier access, existing talent growth and specialization carefully. Later discoveries, ritual formulae and libraries can expand options without giving every Wizard every solution.

**Resources and action costs:** Retain costly scroll learning unless deliberately revised. A ritual's time and ingredients matter only if they create a real expedition decision. Do not add D&D Arcane Recovery to spell-loss casting without specifying exactly what it restores and how often.

**Balance and encounter implications:** Broad known spells already reduce the impact of losing any one spell. Light, access, control, mobility and magical investigation can conserve resources across the entire party. Reliable familiar scouting or unrestricted rituals can compete with Rogue, Ranger and Warlock together.

**Failure and adjudication:** Keep clear spell-loss/mishap procedures. A failed knowledge roll does not make learned languages stop working. Major wards may have discoverable special requirements, not retroactive immunities after the player pays a ritual cost.

**Practical test:** A sequence of different problems, including one where the ideal spell was lost. Observe whether the Wizard still needs allies and whether a new technique changes decisions or merely adds more coverage.

## Why small conversions can have large effects

These are analytic examples under stated assumptions, not playtest data.

- **Casting reliability:** With +2 to a tier-1 casting check (DC 11), success is 12/20 = 60%. Advantage makes it 1 − .4² = 84%. With independent attempts, unchanged modifiers and no recovery, expected successes before the first failure are p/(1−p): **1.5 versus 5.25**. This ignores effects of the spell and critical mishaps, but shows why class-wide advantage is much more than a slight endurance buff. Existing advantage on selected published spells is part of the baseline; it is not automatically removed.
- **Damage resistance:** Against damage types it halves, resistance can approximately double effective HP before healing or rounding. Adding it alongside a higher HP die and armor creates several durability changes at once.
- **Eligibility:** A Rogue feature that works almost every turn is a major damage increase over one that requires an unaware target, even if the extra die stays identical.
- **Actions:** A familiar or summon that independently scouts, attacks or assists may effectively add another party member's opportunities. Its control and information rules are balance rules.
- **Stacking:** A Paladin's party bonus, Bard support and Sorcerer casting benefit may reinforce each other. Check the combined party, not just each entry in isolation. Same-source and different-source stacking limits must be explicit.

## How the classes should differ in play

| Nearest overlap | Distinction to preserve | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Barbarian / Fighter / Monk | Commitment and impact / deliberate control and equipment / disciplined movement and technique | Giving all three equal damage, defense and control with different names |
| Rogue / Ranger | Exploit access, mechanisms and immediate openings / pursue, prepare routes and read expedition threats | Treating stealth as an exclusive permission or Ranger tracking as automatic |
| Bard / Rogue | Magical influence and support / infiltration, tools and opportunism | Bard having all expertise plus a broad full caster kit for free |
| Cleric / Paladin | Broader sacred intervention / martial oath commitments | Paladin receiving complete Fighter and Cleric benefits |
| Druid / Ranger | Transform and shape a living environment / prepare and navigate an expedition | Either replacing the other's identity with unlimited nature utility |
| Wizard / Sorcerer / Warlock | Acquire breadth / shape innate magic / select persistent pact gifts | Same spell list, same acquisition and same recovery with only a different casting stat |

For roughly three PCs, no combination should require a mandatory Cleric, Rogue or Wizard to proceed. Check at least a mostly martial party, a magic-heavy party and a mixed party against the same representative route. NPC help, consumables and alternate approaches can cover gaps without handing every class every answer.

## Priority fixes before numerical tuning

1. **Shared casting contract:** specify casting stat, known spells, learning, ordinary failure, critical failure, recovery, scroll/wand permissions and durations for every magical class. Do not let a new class inherit only the convenient half of Priest/Wizard casting.
2. **Martial operating choices:** Fighter control, Barbarian commitment, Monk techniques and Rogue openings must each work with the basic action structure and feel useful at level 1.
3. **Forms and companions:** define equipment, senses, action costs, light interactions, damage, interruption and replacement before selecting these options.
4. **Healing and support:** inspect resource loops, bonuses to casting and repeatable protection before choosing more HP.
5. **Progression and customization:** draft level-1 sheets and level-2/3 choice menus together. A useful first-three-level milestone does not cap the campaign or settle the later full progression.
6. **Durability and encounters:** only then choose starting HP/recovery and review enemy groups, terrain, attrition and retreat against actual sheets.
