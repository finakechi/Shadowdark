# Shared class design framework

Status: Proposed — SD-020; no revised class is approved or ready for play.  
Date: 2026-10-24 (UTC)  
Scope: the twelve identities in the [accepted roster](class-roster.md). Exact mechanics remain under [HB-002](../rules/homebrew/hb-002-character-options.md).

## Recommendation

Rebuild the twelve classes around a common Shadowdark-compatible standard. Use recognizable identities and review available official Shadowdark examples, then write original adaptations. Do not port twelve D&D rules packages or blindly add the same number of abilities to unequal starting kits.

Noah wants more choices, more magic and meaningful danger. A successful feature changes a decision: where to stand, whom to help, which route to try, what to spend, or what risk to accept. Merely receiving a larger attack bonus does less to address that goal.

## What a level-1 draft must show

These are review requirements, not a mandated count of additional powers.

1. **Identity and operating choice.** Explain what makes this class play differently and one recurring decision it faces.
2. **Useful combat alternatives.** Include a situational alternative to repeating an ordinary attack or the same spell. A spell, stance or maneuver can satisfy this; every class need not gain a separate extra feature.
3. **Expedition or social contribution.** Give a useful way to obtain information, change access, support interaction or manage a risk outside combat. It must work in freeform play without requiring permanent exploration rounds.
4. **Customization.** Offer a manageable early choice. This may be a signature variant, selected technique, spell selection or another bounded choice; avoid separate choices that all select the same result.
5. **Complete chassis.** State HP die (provisional until durability review), weapons, armor, statistics, casting access, talents and advancement. Do not silently inherit contradictory benefits from several systems.
6. **Total capability accounting.** Include baseline features, spells, extra actions, defenses, utility, passive benefits and any summons or forms. Equal feature counts are not proof of equal capability.

Earlier HB-002 proposed two added level-1 features, another at level 2 and a choice/upgrade at level 3. Those numbers remain Proposed. This framework recommends checking complete kits before committing to that additive schedule. It is not an extra layer of benefits on top of it.

## Shared constraints to draft against

- Retain the existing basic action structure initially. No universal bonus action, reaction or extra-attack subsystem is included. Any exception requires its own interaction review.
- Prefer existing opportunity costs: an action, position, equipment commitment, casting risk or a clearly consequential expenditure. Avoid a separate resource pool for every class merely for symmetry.
- At-will choices can be strong when their applicability or tradeoff is meaningful. They need not all carry limited-use counters.
- Casting access counts as capability. A hybrid does not receive a full martial kit plus full caster progression by default.
- More magic is compatible with dangerous exploration. Small effects can be accessible; major bypasses require serious, stated costs. Apply the [HB-003 audit](../rules/homebrew/hb-003-magic-framework.md) to Light, information, translation, curse removal, access, recovery and focus.
- Do not sell back ordinary competence as a feature tax. Preserve plausible improvisation and established class expertise.
- Failure, retries and duration must be explicit. Do not leave unlimited safe retries or indefinite control implicit.
- Short effects must state how they end during freeform exploration; refer to [SD-015](../decisions/decision-log.md#sd-015--combat-initiative-and-situational-noncombat-rounds). Use urgent rounds when a specific immediate danger demands order.
- No class automatically defeats darkness. When light fails in danger, the accepted urgent-round/darkness procedure applies immediately.
- Do not prescribe new HP, recovery or enemy multipliers here. Review durability after capabilities, including damage prevented by control or mobility, are clearer.

## Comparison of adaptation routes

Judgments for this project's stated goals; not empirical measurements. Weighted total = agency × .25 + challenge × .25 + compatibility × .20 + simplicity × .15 + theme × .15.

| Criterion | Weight | Light reskins with minimal new abilities | Coordinated Shadowdark-based redesign | Direct D&D mechanics port |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Agency / meaningful choices | 25% | 3 | 5 | 5 |
| Meaningful challenge | 25% | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| Compatibility / balance | 20% | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Speed / simplicity | 15% | 5 | 4 | 2 |
| Theme / desired experience | 15% | 3 | 5 | 4 |
| Weighted score / 5 | 100% | 3.95 | **4.40** | 3.05 |

Reskins cost less work but risk leaving the feature shortage intact. Coordinated redesign best fits the current objective if abilities stay bounded; it requires more drafting and testing. A direct port offers many choices but brings spell slots, recovery, action types and numerical assumptions that would require much broader changes. A low challenge score means existing Shadowdark threats would need extensive recalibration, not that D&D cannot support difficult play.

These architecture scores replace no earlier decision. The old HB-002 table compares a different set of alternatives. No per-class balance score is credible before rules exist.

## Drafting sequence and completion criteria

1. Draft all twelve level-1 kits against the same checklist, in batches reviewed side by side. Establish a coherent first pass for the full roster before polishing only the likely party.
2. Write level-2/3 progression and explain the intended later direction. Levels 1–3 are an initial design milestone, not an accepted campaign level cap or replacement of Shadowdark's full progression.
3. Audit overlapping roles, spell access, light, healing, scouting, summons/forms, control, duration and retry loops. Compare at least two solutions for significant problems.
4. Select likely characters and deepen those sheets. Finalize guided creation restrictions and permitted sources.
5. Choose durability/recovery and calibrate representative enemy groups, hazards, attrition and retreat opportunities from actual sheets.
6. Move selected drafts to Testing only after Noah chooses that status. Record observations and revisions before Accepted.

For each feature, record: trigger; action/time; target/range; effect; limit and recovery if any; failure/retry; duration/end; interaction with existing rules; what changes from the baseline; and one example of when it is useful or a poor choice.

For each class, compare combat damage and prevention, noncombat options, resources, risk, growth, other classes, GM work, complexity and failure. Use the project rubric on substantial alternatives, explain assumptions, and test at least one combat situation plus one exploration/social situation. Watch for dominant repeatable actions and abilities that never become worth using. No playtest findings exist yet.
