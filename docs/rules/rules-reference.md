## Verified published framework

The publisher's [core description](https://www.thearcanelibrary.com/collections/shadowdark-rpg/products/shadowdark-rpg-pdf) establishes:

- Roll high on a twenty-sided die; resolve tasks with ability checks rather than a separate skill list.
- Six ability scores, generated with 3d6 in order; ancestry and class are separate choices.
- Four core classes: fighter, priest, thief and wizard; class abilities include randomized growth.
- Low hit points, equipment slots, and simple close/near/far distances.
- Roll-to-cast magic, treasure-based experience, monster reactions and morale, and ongoing initiative.
- Player characters lack darkvision.

In everyday terms, hit points measure how much harm a character can withstand; gear slots limit what they can carry; morale and reaction procedures allow encounters to involve hesitation or negotiation. These are short explanations, not replacements for the exact procedures.

The [Quickstart](https://www.thearcanelibrary.com/products/shadowdark-rpg-quickstart-set-pdf) covers levels 1–3 and includes a starting dungeon, pregenerated characters and player/GM materials. Use it to learn the basic loop before building a large optional-rules package.

## Important creator clarifications

The [2024 rules FAQ](https://www.thearcanelibrary.com/blogs/shadowdark-blog/shadowdark-rules-faq) clarifies: STR/DEX affect attack rolls, not normal damage; CON modifies HP at first level rather than each level; light burns in real time during combat too. It advises checks when time pressure, skill and meaningful failure consequences coincide. Its trap discussion protects trained thieves' reliability.

Not everything in that FAQ is a published rule. Delaying actions, a gem-bag allowance and shortening a dying character's timer after further damage are suggestions rather than baseline requirements. Tag such suggestions separately before adopting them.

## Design purpose: evidence and interpretation

[Creator commentary](https://www.thearcanelibrary.com/blogs/shadowdark-blog/all-about-shadowdark-rpg) emphasizes fast, dangerous dungeon exploration, meaningful equipment, quick turns and uncertain magic. The article predates the final core rules; use it as design context, not an errata document.

**Our interpretation:** the game creates challenge through interacting pressures rather than combat statistics alone. Spending time searching can cost light; carrying more equipment competes with recovered treasure; fighting can consume health and magic before the party reaches its objective. A character's useful tool or plan can change the situation without requiring another printed ability.

### Durability and progression

**Published framework:** low HP and randomized class growth are deliberate features, and treasure is a progression incentive (core description).

**Design implication:** extra damage can remove an enemy before it acts, so damage increases can also raise survivability. Reliable escape, recovery and repeated utility magic may alter risk more than modest attack bonuses. Full advancement and HP procedures need checking against the chosen source version before numerical work.

### Exploration and player decisions

**Published framework:** light, carrying capacity, ongoing initiative, reactions and morale support exploration (core description; FAQ).

**Design implication:** show players enough evidence to make informed choices. A dangerous creature can be avoided, bargained with or outmaneuvered. An encounter is not automatically an instruction to fight. Resource pressure is only a meaningful choice when players can understand the tradeoffs.

### Magic and class roles

**Published framework:** casting requires rolls; the four core classes have different functions. The FAQ specifically discusses thieves' trained trap-finding niche.

**Design implication:** extra reliable utility can bypass exploration obstacles or displace another class. Before adding a power, identify whose existing contribution it overlaps with, what it costs and how often it works. Do not import familiar D&D spell-slot or action rules by assumption.

### Downtime and rewards

**Verified creator context:** treasure and carousing, rather than killing enemies, are central to XP incentives (design article).

**Pending:** precise carousing costs/results, learning activities, rest/recovery rules and any newer Western Reaches downtime changes. Keep those versions distinct. Do not invent a campaign advancement schedule.

## Modification sensitivity — recommendations, not official classifications

| Area | Initial sensitivity | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Setting, motives, rumors, encounter goals | Lower | Changes content without automatically altering action or resource rules |
| Original equipment with clear tradeoffs | Moderate | Can add choices, but may affect gear and obstacle solutions |
| Background choices and bounded talent choice | Moderate | Can improve agency; inspect niche overlap and stacking |
| Damage, extra attacks/actions, armor, healing | High | Directly affects enemy opportunities and attrition |
| Darkvision, carrying limits, light duration | High | Can remove connected exploration pressures |
| Spell reliability, free recasts, broad utility | High | May bypass resource limits and entire obstacle categories |
| Guaranteed recovery or easy resurrection | High | Changes consequences and campaign stakes |

These are risk estimates, not prohibitions. Test the smallest useful change first. Preserve meaningful decisions and consequences, not a predetermined death rate.
