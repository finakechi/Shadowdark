| Topic | Verified baseline | Printed pages |

| Fighter | Weapon specialization, a physical expertise choice and carrying capacity support its role; talent results largely add numerical improvements. | 18 |
| Priest | Knows two chosen first-tier spells plus Turn Undead at level 1; later spell knowledge follows its class table. | 20–21 |
| Thief | Trained thievery tasks and advantage support broad competence; Backstab requires the target to be unaware of the attack. | 22 |

| Advancement | Talent rolls occur at levels 1, 3, 5, 7 and 9. Level gains also affect HP and class features/spells where applicable. | 37–38 |

## Implications — interpretation

More talent rolls do not necessarily provide more distinct things to do. A broader action repertoire requires deliberate feature design rather than merely larger numbers.

Magic already has uncertainty and resource consequences through casting failure and critical failure; it is not a spell-slot system. Additional known spells broaden fallback options after failure, which can increase practical endurance even if casting odds do not change.

Preserving torchlight pressure requires examining existing illumination, not only future darkvision or invented cantrips. Likewise, a policy against cheap lock/curse/language bypasses requires spell-by-spell review; adding a noise consequence may not be serious where noise is harmless.

The trained wizard's rune-reading example must not silently disappear. Distinguish recognizing magical notation from knowledge of an unfamiliar language or a deliberately encoded secret; any restriction beyond the source needs an explicit campaign ruling. Do not invalidate learned languages or trained expertise merely to protect a plot.

Remove Curse and Comprehend Languages were Noah's examples of unwanted inexpensive bypasses. This source audit does not claim that those named spells appear in this Quickstart or establish their full-core wording.
