# HB-002 — Expanded character options through class specialties

Status: Proposed

## Problem and baseline

Noah wants characters to have meaningful features, choices and magic from level 1 while retaining Shadowdark's constraints. Three likely players need distinct contributions without mandatory party roles.

The [verified character/magic baseline](../character-magic-baseline.md) maps the uploaded Player Quickstart: classes pp. 18–25, advancement pp. 37–38, checks p. 43. Existing talent rolls often change numbers; additional rolls alone may not address the requested lack of actions.

## Approaches

| Approach | Structure | Benefit | Main cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Baseline classes | Use published talents/features. | Fastest, strongest compatibility. | Leaves the reported feature shortage. |
| B. Existing classes plus chosen specialties — recommended | Keep a recognizable class, add a small set of chosen active/utility features. | Identity and additional decisions without rebuilding every subsystem. | Menus must protect niches and action costs. |
| C. Classless modules | Assemble offense, utility and magic from shared talent selections. | Maximum concept freedom. | Combo checking, role overlap and character-creation burden. |
| D. Broad D&D-style replacement | Import/rebuild fuller class progression and related systems. | Largest immediate repertoire. | Larger action, recovery, spell and encounter redesign. |

These are design routes, not restrictions already imposed on character creation. The twelve-class scope is now selected; exact source permissions, mechanics, ancestry options and subclasses remain unresolved.

## Recommended architecture for review

1. Earlier additive approach: keep the selected class's baseline features, equipment permissions, casting rules and talent schedule initially. For the expanded roster, first audit total capabilities using the shared framework; no class automatically combines a complete D&D kit with these additions.
2. Add **two chosen features at level 1**: one primarily for tactical decisions and one primarily for exploration, information or social contribution.
3. Add **one feature choice at level 2**.
4. At **level 3**, choose one new feature or an upgrade to a previous one, alongside the normal level benefits. Do not assume a level is gained every session or that the campaign must end at level 3.
5. Establish a coherent level-1 first pass for all twelve classes, then level-2/3 choices. Prioritize deeper polishing for the actual party after that roster-wide pass. No full ten-level rewrite is required before the first session.
6. Add no universal bonus action, reaction or extra attack system. Features normally use the existing action or exchange part of an action's normal benefit.
7. Prefer at-will, situational choices paid through opportunity cost or existing resources. Limited-use features need a reason and clear recovery wording; avoid separate pools for each ability.
8. New caster features consume this same feature budget. Do not silently grant two signatures, extra known spells, minor magic and rituals all as independent free additions.
9. No free stat-generation change, multiclassing, HP increase, proficiency expansion or replacement of random talents is included here.

The numerical feature count is proposed. Menus and exact rules are deliberately not finalized before this architecture is selected.

## Concrete directions for the first menus — not rule text

| Class | Tactical direction | Exploration / social direction | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fighter | Exchange some damage opportunity for movement, protection or weapon control. | Assess fortifications, organize a safe crossing, or recognize signs of military occupation. | A fighter specialty improves reliability or effect; others can still attempt plausible physical actions. |
| Thief | Use positioning, distraction or escape to support a later attack. | Infiltration, mechanisms, routes and underworld contacts. | Do not grant automatic invisibility, immunity to traps or universal successful deception. |
| Priest | Protective or restorative choices that compete with other actions/resources. | Interpret a spiritual trace, contain a symptom, or establish a limited sanctuary. | No free curse removal, indefinite safe rest, unlimited healing or guaranteed divine answers. |
| Wizard | Shape delivery, placement or limited utility of a spell rather than adding extra casting actions. | Study magical structures, acquire bounded minor magic, or access specified rituals. | No automatic comprehensive translation, perpetual light or unbounded obstacle bypass. |

These illustrate meaningful differences. A mundane fighter and a relic-using fighter are both possible directions; neither supernatural martial powers nor a purely mundane campaign is assumed.

Do not make existing expertise into a purchasable feature tax. In particular, thieves already have strong trained competence and wizards already have a rune-reading example in the baseline. A new feature must add something rather than sell back what they could already do.

## Interaction audit and test plan

- Combat and survivability: control/protection may prevent enemy actions even without raising HP. Review forced movement, repeated control and protection combinations.
- Utility/resources: new options can save spells, gear and time. Preserve consequences rather than making every feature require a new tracker.
- Encounter risk: improved access and information can legitimately bypass selected encounters; adapt locations rather than canceling all new powers.
- Progression: these choices are additive to baseline growth. Inspect the level-3 spike and caster/noncaster differences.
- Class overlap: each character should have distinct strong contributions, but no obstacle requires a specific missing class.
- GM burden/complexity: target short feature cards; compare actual adjudication burden after menu drafting.
- Failure: specify result, repeatability and consequences; no open-ended roll-until-success loop.
- Test: after a menu is drafted and Noah selects Testing, include a tactical situation and an exploration/social obstacle for each feature. Record repeated dominant choices and sidelined abilities.
- Rollback: remove only these added features at an agreed pause; retain earned levels, baseline talents and prior fictional outcomes. Do not force a character rebuild mid-scene.
