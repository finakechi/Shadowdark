# HB-003 — Expanded magic with bounded utility and consequential rituals

Status: Proposed  
Version: 0.1 — framework and audit plan; not a playable replacement spell list  
Created / updated: 2026-10-23  
Related decision: SD-017 (Proposed)  
Approval / playtest evidence: none

## Objective and baseline

Increase magical variety without cheap, universal answers to important obstacles, free permanent light, or a return to routine exploration rounds.

The [source map](../character-magic-baseline.md) cites the uploaded Player Quickstart. Casting currently uses an action and a check against 10 + tier, with spell loss on ordinary failure and additional critical consequences (pp. 52–54). Priest/wizard starting spell choices differ (pp. 20–25). Focus has turn-based upkeep (p. 56).

Existing utility already matters: Detect Magic (p. 60), Knock and Light (p. 62). Review existing effects as well as additions. The named Remove Curse / Comprehend Languages examples express a design preference; their presence or full-core wording has not been established here.

## Approaches compared

| Route | Structure | Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Baseline magic | Keep current lists and checks. | Familiar, little new administration. | Existing utility and focus conflicts remain. |
| B. Expand known spells without utility review | More choices on the existing engine. | Immediate breadth. | More fallback spells and bypasses may reduce attrition and invalidate obstacles. |
| C. Preserve casting core; audit utility and add specified minor magic/rituals — recommended | Retain roll-to-cast initially; expand by bounded effects and explicit costs. | More magic with clearer adventure consequences. | Requires a real spell audit and duration wording. |
| D. Unified mana pool | Pay points for a broad list of effects. | Flexible expenditure. | Another resource and a major endurance/recovery redesign. |

## Recommended framework for review

### 1. Retain the casting engine provisionally

Do not change casting ability, spell DC, action cost, ordinary failure, critical consequences or recovery in this proposal. This is a recommended starting point, not a declaration that every future complaint about spell loss is invalid.

Additional known spells alter endurance by providing alternatives after a spell is lost. Count expanded spell knowledge as a substantive feature, not a free addition on top of every class upgrade.

### 2. Use three authoring categories, without a new point currency

**Minor magic:** small, bounded effects that provide useful color or assistance. They may manipulate a mark, a small sensory detail or an existing object, but do not supply unlimited illumination, combat damage, remote trap manipulation, perfect scouting or automatic broad translation by default. Do not prohibit clever incidental uses merely because they are useful. Each actual trick needs a written scope.

A candidate navigation mark could be visible only in existing light and placed by touch. This is a sketch, not a granted ability or a finished spell. Minor magic can be a chosen specialty within HB-002's budget; it is not automatically added to every character.

**Adventuring spells:** effects for fights, traversal and limited problem solving. Keep their important limits and costs visible. Avoid describing every useful effect as forbidden; some problems should become easier because characters have magic.

**Major rituals:** effects that comprehensively remove a major obstacle, lift a significant curse or reveal a complete protected text. Each ritual needs a specific target/scope, duration, prerequisites, effect, consumed cost and interruption/failure wording. Rituals are not a blanket permission to improvise any requested result.

### 3. Define serious costs through an actual choice

Possible cost models:
- A rare reagent acquired through play and consumed; it cannot be replenished simply by resting.
- An irreplaceable relic with several competing uses; spending it on translation means it cannot later break a seal.
- A stated obligation or bargain with a credible consequence.
- Substantial time in an exposed location, combined with another cost when time alone would be harmless.

A universal price list is not yet adopted. Avoid trivial gold costs, hidden post-payment limitations and automatic permanent stat damage as the default. Match severity to effect and reveal the terms before commitment.

A paid ritual should deliver its stated result when its written requirements are met, or disclose the exact risk of failure before payment. Do not ask for an undefined rare cost and then protect the plot with an undisclosed immunity. Unique wards or curses can have known requirements, discoverable through play.

### 4. Audit the existing list before promising restrictions

| Effect | Why review it | Candidate directions to compare |
|---|---|---|
| Light | Can replace carried illumination when available; casting risk alone may or may not preserve desired scarcity. | Keep baseline; require a consumed component; or provide a constrained alternative. No change chosen yet. |
| Knock | Opens mundane locks/barriers; noise matters only when the location makes it matter. | Keep and honor the loud consequence; limit the effect's scope; or move comprehensive opening to a costly ritual. |
| Detect Magic | Useful investigation that currently needs focus upkeep. | Preserve useful information while writing a bounded exploration procedure. |
| Invisibility / mobility / control | Can bypass patrols, terrain or enemy opportunities. | Preserve selected successes, specify limits and adjust scenarios without blanket immunity. |
| Rune/language competence | Wizards have existing trained competence, and learned languages should matter. | Distinguish notation, unfamiliar language and deliberate cipher explicitly. Any new restriction needs a stated ruling. |

None of these spells is automatically banned, rewritten or weakened by this audit.

### 5. Resolve durations without forcing continuous exploration rounds

SD-015 governs: ordinary exploration is freeform, combat uses initiative, and noncombat rounds need a specific timing-sensitive purpose.

Compare three approaches:
1. Preserve all published short timing, using local rounds only where an effect matters. Highest mechanical compatibility, but can retain the bookkeeping problem.
2. Write **separate exploration applications** for selected utility spells, with bounded targets and explicit time/termination conditions. Recommended direction to develop.
3. Convert all round durations to a fixed number of minutes. Easy to remember but unsafe as a blanket rule: control, invisibility, damage-over-time and protection may become much stronger.

Illustration for approach 2: develop a deliberate Detect Magic examination that takes stated time and reveals bounded information. Establish whether the initial casting check remains, what interruption does, whether movement is allowed and whether a transition to combat ends or changes that application. This is a design brief, not an approved substitute for focus.

Do not silently waive combat focus checks, extend effects across an entire expedition or count every unrelated conversation as a round. Spell-by-spell wording is required; a general scene-length conversion is not approved.

## Interactions, test and rollback

- Combat power: additional knowledge, utility and control can improve survival and conserve other spells. Do not assess only damage.
- Noncombat: complete bypasses can be appropriate rewards if their access/cost is meaningful; ordinary clues must still have accessible routes.
- Resources: count the practical frequency of casting attempts, spell loss, healing and illumination; avoid a free new refresh mechanism.
- Encounters: compare what can now be avoided or disabled, then adapt selected objectives and environments rather than negate all magic.
- Progression: connect expanded knowledge/ritual access to chosen features or earned discoveries; no automatic every-spell access.
- Niches: protect learned languages, thief expertise and nonmagical solutions. Do not sell existing competence back as a new feature.
- GM burden: prepare explicit ritual offers and outcomes; avoid improvised prices changing mid-scene.
- Failure: disclose interruption, loss and reattempt conditions. No undefined ritual mishap table is added.
- Testing: after exact spell text and approval, include a language/cipher problem, a barrier, illumination and an urgent focus situation. Compare expected to actual choices and tracking burden.
- Rollback: restore individual baseline spells at a stated transition; preserve prior discovered information and completed story outcomes. Agree any inventory compensation before changing already-bought components. No test has occurred.
