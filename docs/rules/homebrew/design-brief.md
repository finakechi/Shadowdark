# Initial homebrew design brief

Status: design exploration only. No mechanics accepted or active tests. Governing decisions: SD-002, SD-004, SD-007, SD-008, SD-009, SD-011 and SD-012 in the [decision log](../../decisions/decision-log.md).

## Desired outcome

More things worth doing with a character, during both combat and exploration, while retaining meaningful underground risk and many Shadowdark constraints. Start at level 1 and allow growth. D&D 2024 is a capability reference and its twelve PHB class identities are now the selected roster (SD-019). Exact numerical durability, recovery, action economy and class mechanics remain unresolved.

Noah identified bookkeeping as the central out-of-combat turn frustration, especially with few abilities worth using. More features, choices and magic are explicit goals. Preserve torchlight pressure for the most part; major magical bypasses must have serious costs if available. Greater toughness is open for evaluation, with appropriately dangerous enemies/hazards. Do not presume that either issue is solved by extra damage or hit points. Do not require an unmodified pilot as an already-agreed condition.

## First track: exploration flow

The [exploration baseline](../exploration-baseline.md) now verifies the relevant printed pages. [HB-001 v0.1](hb-001-exploration-flow.md) is a complete Proposed procedure, with official freeform and group-round alternatives, rubric scores, timing exceptions and a rollback plan. No version has been selected for Testing.

| Candidate direction | Potential benefit | Risk to examine |
|---|---|---|
| Conversational exploration with periodic group time accounting | Natural discussion, fewer forced handoffs | Dominant voices and unnoticed time/resource drift |
| Simultaneous declarations at a shared exploration checkpoint | Everyone contributes and costs are visible | Can become another administrative ritual |
| Retain turn structure but improve task scope and character utility | May preserve pressure while addressing narrow actions | May not solve the waiting/flow frustration |

These are alternative directions, not rules. Bookkeeping is now the confirmed priority; conversational exploration with minimal shared tracking is the initial recommendation to develop, not an adopted procedure. A replacement must specify how time advances, when hazards/encounters are checked, how lengthy tasks compare, how party splitting works and how quiet players receive spotlight. Preserve danger through transparent time and consequences rather than an unexplained procedure.

## Second track: character capability

The [twelve-class roster](../../characters/class-roster.md) records accepted scope and proposed identity briefs. The [shared framework](../../characters/class-design-framework.md) compares reskins, coordinated redesign and direct porting. Assess total capability, including spells; do not silently add identical feature counts to every new class.

[HB-002](hb-002-character-options.md) now compares baseline classes, class specialties, classless modules and broader replacement. The [character/magic baseline](../character-magic-baseline.md) supplies printed source references. Feature menus and final counts are not approved.

| Candidate direction | Potential benefit | Risk to examine |
|---|---|---|
| Add a few distinctive class-specific choices | Identity and useful options without a large rules layer | Niche overlap, stacking and new default actions |
| Add bounded shared maneuvers plus class specialties | Gives everyone situational tools | Replaces imaginative action with a restrictive menu |
| Broader class progression and more robust starting characters | More substantially changes the experience | Durability, recovery, action economy and prep all need calibration |

Options and toughness are separate axes; different combinations are possible. Inspect utility, encounter bypass, resource costs, action count, failure consequences and role overlap before judging success. Define useful noncombat capabilities as carefully as attacks.

## Third track: magic that supports adventure

[HB-003](hb-003-magic-framework.md) now compares magic architectures, audits existing utility and identifies the focus/freeform interaction. It contains no approved replacement spells.

Accepted direction: more magic, but no cheap, immediate removal of major obstacles. The examples below are assistant design sketches, not official spell descriptions, scored proposals or campaign rules.

| Problem | Accessible magical assistance to explore | Major solution to evaluate separately |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown inscription | Reveal a theme, symbol connection or limited fragment | Full translation with a rare consumed reagent, meaningful exposure or a consequential bargain |
| Curse | Reveal a clue to origin, temporarily contain one symptom, or identify a remedy | Removal through a specific ritual, scarce sacrifice or an adventure objective |
| Sealed magical site | Locate an energy source or expose a weakness | Open it by spending an irreplaceable expedition asset or accepting a clearly stated lasting cost |

Do not make every small magical convenience expensive. Classify effects by what they bypass and how reliably they do so. A cost should create a meaningful decision in this expedition, be stated before commitment and be trackable without a new subsystem. Time is only a serious cost when pressure makes it consequential. A unique item, pact or permanent loss can be serious, but severity and exact wording require explicit proposal review.

Avoid designing plot-critical secrets with only one route forward. Magic may reward preparation and solve selected problems; it should not require immunity clauses on every obstacle to preserve the campaign.

## Durability and threat calibration — recommendations

[HB-004](hb-004-durability-and-encounters.md) now compares starting HP options and provides a conditional encounter-calibration plan. Its formula and recommendations are Proposed, not adopted.

Treat action count, HP, recovery and defense as separate adjustments. Compare a modest survival buffer, broader D&D-like toughness, and improved defensive options before recommending a package. Keep consequences and resource expenditure visible.

Do not multiply all enemy damage by the same factor as player HP and call that improved resilience. Stronger enemies, demanding hazards and dangerous choices can coexist with characters surviving some mistakes that previously would have ended them. Review time-to-defeat, incapacitation risk, utility bypasses and recovery across an expedition, not only one combat. No threat multiplier or HP rule is adopted.

## Adventure implications — recommendations

For around three characters, test how roles and available actions cover the selected obstacles; do not assume a fourth player or prescribe mandatory classes. Prepare multiple approaches to essential objectives.

Build a bounded route in a larger underground location rather than fully detailing a vast megadungeon. Use factions and inhabitants to support social play, and distinct environmental situations for puzzles and exploration. Let improved character abilities solve some problems; keep meaningful decisions elsewhere. Do not increase every obstacle to cancel the new abilities.

Compare official, original and hybrid adventure approaches after identifying likely mechanical changes. Existing modules may need targeted changes to light/time pressures, traversal, encounters and recovery opportunities; the extent is not yet measured.

## Proposal gate

For any significant candidate, write exact procedures and limits, compare baseline and at least two plausible alternatives, use the weighted rubric, identify uncertainties and set a test/rollback plan. Scores remain unknown until a concrete rule and campaign assumptions exist. Noah chooses Testing or Accepted status.
