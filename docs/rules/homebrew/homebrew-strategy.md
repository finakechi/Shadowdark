# Homebrew design strategy

Project preference: [SD-002](../../decisions/decision-log.md#sd-002--player-options-and-meaningful-challenge).
Goal: increase power, flexibility or utility where worthwhile while retaining informed risk and consequential decisions. This is a direction, not a guarantee that every proposed increase is compatible.

## Procedure

1. State a specific observed frustration or desired experience.
2. Verify the baseline rule, exact source revision and relevant official clarifications.
3. Compare baseline with at least two plausible alternatives for significant changes.
4. Document effects on combat, utility, resources, encounter risk, progression, other classes, GM prep, complexity, and failure probability/consequences.
5. Score with the framework below. State evidence quality and assumptions; use unknown where necessary.
6. Noah selects whether to test. Change one connected mechanic at a time when practical.
7. Review actual decisions, pacing, resource use and class contributions; keep, revise or reject the experiment.

## Weighted evaluation

| Criterion | Weight | A score of 1 means | A score of 5 means |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Player agency and meaningful options | 25% | Few useful choices or one dominant choice | Several understandable, viable choices |
| Preservation of meaningful challenge | 25% | Removes important risks without replacement | Retains informed risk and costly tradeoffs |
| System compatibility and balance | 20% | Breaks niches, stacking or connected procedures | Fits existing systems and roles |
| Speed and simplicity of play | 15% | Frequent lookups/bookkeeping/slow turns | Quick to understand and adjudicate |
| Theme and intended experience | 15% | Conflicts with agreed tone | Strongly supports agreed tone |

Use integers 1–5; 2–4 are intermediate judgments. Weighted overall **out of 5** = 0.25A + 0.25C + 0.20B + 0.15S + 0.15T. If a percentage display helps, multiply by 20 and label it a rescaling, not a measured success probability. Do not hide a fatal interaction behind a high average. Leave theme unscored until the campaign tone is defined; do not silently redistribute its weight.

Arithmetic demonstration only: scores 4,3,4,4,3 produce 3.60/5. This is not an evaluation of an actual Shadowdark modification. Baseline receives the same rubric and assumptions as each alternative.

## Prefer meaningful choices

Recommendation: begin with options that exchange benefits rather than add unconditional bonuses. An illustrative maneuver could trade an attack's damage opportunity for creating an escape route. This is **not a drafted or adopted rule**; exact actions, check procedure, effect, limits and interactions require a proposal.

Alternative approaches to character agency:
- Offer a limited choice among fitting talent results: more direction, but inspect how repeatedly selecting one result stacks.
- Offer training or equipment opportunities in play: ties utility to treasure, time or faction obligations.
- Improve clarity and consistent adjudication of actions already possible: may resolve frustration without a new mechanic.

## Main mechanical risks

Extra actions can multiply both damage and utility. Damage gains can shorten enemies' lives and prevent retaliation. Armor, healing and recovery can erase attrition. Universal stealth, locks or trap abilities can displace thieves. Darkvision, unlimited carrying and easy light can remove exploration tradeoffs. Broad spell reliability can bypass obstacles. Progression changes can compress the intended risk curve. Track failure consequences and noncombat bypasses as carefully as damage.

If characters become stronger, first ask which specific challenges changed. Adjust objectives, enemy tactics, terrain, time pressure or resource access in proportion to the observed effect. Do not reflexively add monster HP or punish successful use of a new ability. Some obstacles should become easier as the reward for character growth; retain uncertainty elsewhere.

## Playtest evidence

Use [balance notes](../../playtesting/balance-notes.md). Compare similar situations where possible; note differences in party size, preparation, dice and GM choices. Small samples support tentative conclusions only. Record successful clever play separately from accidental mechanical dominance. No fixed death quota or universal encounter multiplier is assumed.
